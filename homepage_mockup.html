Current Thinking

Strategic Bombing and Industrial Development

Research Paper • March 2024 • 8,400 words • Evergreen

Using newly digitized damage assessments from the U.S. Strategic Bombing Survey, I examine how the destruction of Japanese industrial capacity during WWII shaped postwar economic geography. The data reveals surprising patterns of resilience and path dependence in manufacturing clusters.

The Craft of Data Janitorial Work

Essay • February 2024 • 3,200 words • Budding

Why the unglamorous work of cleaning historical datasets—correcting OCR errors, standardizing geographic names, reconciling conflicting sources—is where real insights begin. A meditation on methodology disguised as a technical tutorial.

Border Markets and State Formation

Essay • January 2024 • 5,600 words • Evergreen

Cross-border trade between Thailand and Burma in the 1970s operated through informal networks that both challenged and reinforced state authority. Drawing on customs records and trader interviews, this piece maps the political economy of smuggling in mainland Southeast Asia.

From the Research Lab

Manufacturing Revolutions in Southeast Asia

Working Paper • Updated January 2024 • Budding

Tracing the movement of textile production from Japan to Thailand, Malaysia, and Indonesia between 1960–1990. This paper combines firm-level data with oral histories to understand how tacit knowledge travels across borders.

State Formation in the Mekong Delta

Research Note • December 2023 • Seedling

Early explorations into cadastral records from French colonial Vietnam. The maps reveal competing visions of territorial control that persist in contemporary land disputes.

My work lives at the intersection of computational methods and historical inquiry. I write in R, Python, and occasionally Stata, always with an eye toward reproducible research. The tools shape the questions as much as the questions shape the tools. Current obsessions include spatial econometrics, OCR optimization, and the poetry of clean data pipelines.
